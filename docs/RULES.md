# Rules (these outrank everything else, including the person running the kit)

1. **A human sends, or a human says yes.** Claude may search, read profiles, click Connect or Message, type text and create email drafts. It stops before the final Send and shows the exact text. It sends only after a clear yes for that one message, or the person clicks it. One yes never covers the next send.
2. **The human handles accounts.** Sign-ins, passwords, 2FA, CAPTCHAs, "verify it's you" screens, and any LinkedIn limit or "unusual activity" notice all go to the person. When any of these appear, the session stops.
3. **Truth.** Messages say only true things. Facts about the sender and the offer come from `private/SPEC.md`. Facts about the recipient come from a source you read this session. Every score signal above 0 needs an evidence URL. Never invent traction, credentials, mutual connections, quotes or urgency.
4. **Human pace, no evasion.** Volume and spacing stay at what one busy person does by hand (`python -m outreach pace` / `check`). No proxies, fingerprint spoofing, simulated mouse or typing, account rotation or CAPTCHA solving. LinkedIn's User Agreement prohibits automated messaging, so approving every send at human volume is what keeps the risk low. It is not zero, and the person should know that.
5. **Public data only.** Emails come from places the person published them: their site, the company site, a public profile, a public post. A guessed address or a paid enrichment tool needs the person's explicit decision recorded in the spec, and guessed addresses are marked `email_source: guessed`. Never verify an address by sending probe emails. Never scrape private data.
6. **Respect no.** An opt-out, "unsubscribe", "not interested" or a bounce adds the person to the do-not-contact list (`log ... optout` does this automatically). They're never contacted again on any channel.
7. **Email law.** Emails carry the sender's real name, organization and postal address, and a working opt-out line (US CAN-SPAM). For EU/UK recipients: a legitimate-interest reason, a clear way to object, and deletion on request (GDPR).
8. **No duplicates.** Run `find` before adding anyone. `add` refuses duplicates. Lint refuses near-identical messages to two people at the same company.
9. **Log only what happened,** and only after verifying it (the thread, Sent invitations, the Pending button, or the email's Sent folder).
10. **Private stays private.** `config.json`, `data/` and `private/` are git-ignored. Never commit or publish them. They hold other people's personal data.
